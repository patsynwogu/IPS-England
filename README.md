# IPS-England

An agent-based model of the statutory pathway a child in England travels from an unmet special
educational need for an Education, Health and Care (EHC) plan.

**Live page:** https://spontaneous-dasik-54b8ae.netlify.app/
**Version:** 1.1, September 2026

## What This Is

IPS-England represents individual children moving through five stages of the SEND identification
and assessment pathway, across all 152 local authorities, each with its own caseload and its own
assessment capacity. It runs on a weekly time step over a six-year horizon.

The model was built to represent the standard explanation for assessment delay in England, which
is a shortage of assessment staff. The intention was to reproduce England from that assumption and
then use the working model to examine different resourcing decisions. It could not reproduce
England at any staffing level. Everything else here follows from that failure.

## The Finding

England, calendar year 2025: 162,702 requests for an EHC needs assessment, 110,708 new plans
issued, 46.1% of them within the 20-week statutory deadline. Any explanation of delay has to
generate all three figures at once.

**Account A, delay as a queue.** Sweeping assessment capacity across the full plausible range
produces no setting that reaches England. Match timeliness at 45.2%, and the model issues 93,384
plans a year, 15.6% short. Match volume and timeliness climb to 93.2%, forty-seven points above
what England achieves. The two targets move in opposite directions, and the gap never closes.

**Account B, delay as process duration.** Hold capacity at a level where queues stay short and
allow the statutory process itself to take longer. At an average duration of 21.5 weeks with
realistic variation between cases, the model issues 108,964 plans a year with 46.0% inside the
deadline, against England's 110,708 and 46.1%.

The model reaches England only when the assessment process takes longer than the deadline allows.

## The Hypothesis

Assessment delay in England is generated by the duration of the mandated statutory sequence rather
than by assessment capacity. Increases in caseworker numbers raise throughput without materially
reducing time to plan.

**How to falsify it.** Identify local authorities that materially increased assessment staffing,
then compare the change in their throughput against the change in their 20-week performance. The
hypothesis predicts throughput rose and timeliness did not follow. A second route is a direct audit
of how long each mandated step takes in practice, which would settle the question without any
simulation.

## Reproducing the Results
pip install numpy
python3 model.py


This regenerates `sweep.json`. Python is authoritative; the page reads that file and performs no
computation of its own, so the published figures and the model cannot drift apart.

## Data

| Source | Period | Used For |
|---|---|---|
| DfE, *Education, health and care plans* | Calendar 2025, published January 2026 | The three targets: requests, plans issued, share within 20 weeks |
| DfE, *Special educational needs in England* | 2015/16 to 2025/26 | The eleven-year identification trend, which rose from 14.4% to 20.8% of pupils |
| Ministry of Justice, *Tribunal Statistics* | 2024/25 | Appeal volume and outcome, cited for context and not used in the model |

The two DfE series count different populations and are not interchangeable. The plans release
covers ages 0 to 25 across every setting and reports 718,838 plans in force at January 2026. The
schools census covers school pupils only and reports 538,547 with plans in 2025/26.

## Parameters

Measured, derived, swept and assumed values are kept separate throughout, since the difference
determines how much weight a result can carry.

| Parameter | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Local authorities | 152 | Measured |
| Statutory deadline | 20 weeks | Measured, set in regulation |
| Requests for assessment | 162,702 per year | Measured |
| Request-to-plan conversion | 68.0% | Derived from two published flows in one calendar year; an approximation |
| Assessment capacity | 0.70 to 1.62 times demand | Swept, Account A |
| Statutory process duration | 8 to 26.5 weeks | Swept, Account B |
| Variation in process duration | Gamma, CV 0.45 | Assumed |
| Caseload distribution across authorities | Lognormal, sigma 0.65 | Assumed |
| Capacity distribution across authorities | Lognormal, sigma 0.45 | Assumed |
| Time step and horizon | Weekly, six years, final year reported | Design choice |

## Validation

**Sensitivity.** Account A's failure turns on process duration, so that dependence is reported
rather than buried. Holding capacity adequate, timeliness runs 83.3% at a 10-week process, 82.6%
at 14 weeks, 78.6% at 18, 70.7% at 20, and 46.0% at 21.5 weeks with variation. No short-process
configuration reaches England.

**Scale audit.** The same configuration is run at several agent resolutions and the outputs
compared. Anything that moves when resolution changes describes the code rather than England. This
test has caught real errors twice in earlier versions, both withdrawn: a timeliness figure that
proved to be a discretisation artefact, and an authority-level ratio that proved to be Monte Carlo
noise. Neither appears on the page. The same class of error resurfaced during the current rebuild,
where per-authority monthly capacity was floored to a whole number each month instead of carrying
its remainder forward, and was caught the same way.

## Limitations

Authority-level caseload and capacity dispersion are modelled distributions rather than the
published per-authority breakdown, so no claim should be drawn from this model about variation
between individual authorities.

The 68.0% request-to-plan conversion is a same-year ratio. Requests late in the year produce plans in
the next, so the true cohort conversion differs by an unknown margin.

Variation in process duration between cases is assumed rather than measured.

The stage that brings children into the pathway is calibrated to the national trend, and its
absolute level is an assumption. Nothing here is a claim about which children enter the pathway, or
about variation in identification between groups.

This model addresses delay, not outcome. A plan issued within 20 weeks is recorded as a success,
and whether the provision that followed helped the child is a question no national dataset answers.
